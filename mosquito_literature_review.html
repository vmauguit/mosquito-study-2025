Mosquito thermal ecology
& climate change — literature review

Supporting references for the correlation between surface water temperature (Tmargin),
adult mosquito catch, and KNMI Hd warming scenario implications.

Core argument: Surface water temperature in reed margins predicts adult Cx. pipiens catch (ρ = 0.90, lag 1 wk) and An. maculipennis catch (ρ = 0.95, lag 2 wk), consistent with thermal development thresholds documented in the literature below. Under the KNMI Hd scenario (+0.81 °C by 2036), summer temperatures approach the documented transmission optimum of 24.5 °C for Cx. pipiens.
Temperature & transmission — mechanistic models
★ Cited on poster
Shocket, M.S., Verwillow, A.B., Numazu, M.G., Slamani, H., Cohen, J.M., El Moustaid, F., Rohr, J., Johnson, L.R., & Mordecai, E.A. (2020). Transmission of West Nile and five other temperate mosquito-borne viruses peaks at temperatures between 23–26 °C. eLife, 9:e58511. https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.58511
Bayesian trait-based R₀ models for 10 vector–virus pairs including Cx. pipiens × WNV. Transmission optimum: 24.5 °C; lower thermal limit: 16.8 °C; upper limit: 34.9 °C. Validated with US county-level WNV case data peaking at 24 °C. Infection efficiency (c) and pathogen development rate curves directly support the interpretation of KNMI Hd warming projections in this study.
Thermal
Ciota, Matacchiero, Kilpatrick & Kramer (2014). The effect of temperature on life history traits of Culex mosquitoes. Journal of Medical Entomology, 51(1), 55–62.
Experimental validation of developmental rates, thermal limits, and daily mortality under shifting temperature baselines in Cx. pipiens and related species. Key source for degree-day parameterisation.
Thermal
Vogels, Göertz, Pijlman & Koenraadt (2017). Vector competence of northern and southern European Cx. pipiens pipiens for West Nile virus across a gradient of temperatures. Medical and Veterinary Entomology, 31(4), 358–364.
Critical Dutch/European study showing WNV vector competence varies with temperature in northern Cx. pipiens. Extrinsic incubation period shortens markedly above 18 °C. Directly relevant to predicted warming in Zuid-Holland.
Target species biology
Biology
Becker et al. (2010). Mosquitoes and Their Control (2nd ed.). Springer, Berlin.
Core authority for lifecycle timelines, degree-day thresholds, and anatomical diagnostic traits across European Culicidae including Cx. pipiens, An. maculipennis, and Cx. modestus.
Biology
Fonseca et al. (2004). Emerging vectors in the Culex pipiens complex. Science, 303(5663), 1535–1538.
Foundational genetics paper establishing evolutionary splits and hybridisation potential between Cx. pipiens f. pipiens and f. molestus. Relevant to understanding bridge-vector capacity.
Biology
Balenghien et al. (2008). Evidence of laboratory vector competence of Culex modestus for West Nile virus. Journal of the American Mosquito Control Association, 24(2), 318–320.
Definitive experimental proof of Cx. modestus as a highly efficient WNV bridge vector — a species regularly trapped at Eendragtspolder and listed as a primary WNV vector in European surveillance frameworks.
Biology
Takken et al. (2002). Distribution and dynamics of larval populations of An. messeae and An. atroparvus in the delta of the rivers Rhine and Meuse. Ambio, 31(3), 212–218.
Modern baseline mapping of Dutch Anopheles maculipennis complex species; salinity gradients, larval competition, and microclimate partitioning across the Dutch delta — directly analogous to Eendragtspolder habitat.
Netherlands — surveillance & risk
NL
RIVM (2022). Muggen in Nederland: Monitoring en risicobeoordeling. RIVM Rapport 2022-0122. Bilthoven.
National regulatory reference for species distribution and arboviral threat assessment in the Netherlands. Provides current surveillance framework context for this study.
NL
Rijks et al. (2016). Widespread Usutu virus outbreak in birds in the Netherlands 2016. Eurosurveillance, 21(45), 30388.
Documents the 2016 Dutch avian Usutu epizootic, directly implicating native Culex vectors. Demonstrates that warming-linked vector expansion has already produced documented arboviral emergence in the Netherlands.
NL
Stroo, Buijs & Takken (2016). Abundance and distribution of mosquitoes in the Netherlands in relation to land use. Medical and Veterinary Entomology, 30(2), 228–239.
Field trap evaluation of Dutch species across urban polders, marshlands, and woodlands — directly relevant to interpreting BG/GT catch data from Eendragtspolder within national context.
NL
Swellengrebel & de Buck (1938). Malaria in the Netherlands. Scheltema & Holkema, Amsterdam.
Historical record of endemic Plasmodium vivax transmission in Dutch coastal polders by An. atroparvus. Establishes long-term baseline for Anopheles risk in the same landscape type as Eendragtspolder.
Climate change & vector-borne disease
Climate
Medlock & Leach (2015). Effect of climate change on vector-borne disease risk in the UK. The Lancet Infectious Diseases, 15(6), 721–730.
High-impact climate modelling assessing floodplain and wet-woodland systems in Northwestern Europe under warming scenarios. Directly comparable climate context to Zuid-Holland polder systems.
Climate
Schrama et al. (2023). Human activities elevate mosquito-borne disease risk by reshaping co-occurring host communities. PNAS, 120(4), e2215028120.
Evaluates how drainage structures and land-use shifts in wet polders alter vector host communities — directly relevant to Eendragtspolder as a managed retention reservoir in an agricultural landscape.
Climate
ECDC (2019). Guidelines for the Surveillance of Native Mosquitoes in Europe. ECDC Technical Document. Stockholm.
Continental framework standardising trap placement, species identification, and reporting for European mosquito surveillance programmes. Contextualises GT and BG trap methodology used in this study.
